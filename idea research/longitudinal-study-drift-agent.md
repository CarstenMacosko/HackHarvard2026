# Build the agent that knows what changed

The strongest Regeneron entry here is not a general "AI for long studies". It is a **version-aware monitoring agent**: it knows which protocol version, lab method, coding dictionary and site roster applied to every data point, and when something changes it works out which collected data, review checks and past answers are now wrong. Regeneron's own **Signal, Not Noise** sheet already asks for this in its top ambition rung, "Stay current". **Trial by Design Part 3** (versioned single source of truth) and **Caught in 4K** (deviation checks that respect amendment effective dates) ask for the same building block, so the team can say truthfully that it ties three prompts together. The problem is real. **76% of protocols now carry at least one substantial amendment**, and **sites run on mismatched protocol versions for an average of 215 days** ([IntuitionLabs summary of Tufts CSDD](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/clinical-trial-protocol-amendments-cost-data)). Silent measurement changes do comparable damage. An MRI scanner swap can fake a year of Alzheimer's atrophy ([Lee et al. 2019](https://adni.loni.usc.edu/adni-publications/Estimating%20and%20accounting%20for%20the%20effect%20of%20MRI%20scanner%20changes%20on%20longitudinal%20whole-brain%20volume%20change%20measurements.pdf)), and a creatinine assay switch put false step changes into 23.7% of UK eGFR series ([Poh et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.1542)). The agent helps most in three settings: 15-year gene and cell therapy follow-up, long neurodegeneration and imaging studies, and any multi-year trial that goes through amendments and MedDRA up-versions. For a 36-hour build, the team should take CDISC pilot SDTM data, replay it over three simulated years with planted drift events and an answer key, and demo one moment. Amendment 3 lands, 40 false lab alerts collapse into one "unit change, not clinical" card, and the real slow creatinine trend still surfaces, scored against a naive baseline.

## Regeneron already asks for this under the name "stay current"

None of the Regeneron starter sheets uses the phrase "longitudinal drift". At least four of them ask for its core building block: rules that know their version, and a system that knows what changed since last time. The best fit is **Signal, Not Noise (medical monitoring)**, and the fit is close. The sheet lists "re-reviewing the unchanged" and "slow trends below thresholds, e.g., creeping creatinine at one site" as pain points. It counts mg/dL vs µmol/L unit artifacts as noise that belongs with data management. Its top ambition rung reads: **"When the protocol is amended, show which review checks and which past answers must change, and update them with approval."** It also describes the data the demo needs: daily or weekly data cuts over simulated time, signals that develop over several visits, a mid-trial amendment that changes an eligibility rule and a dosing rule, and a locked treatment-arm file ([Regeneron HackHarvard kit](https://regn.link/hackharvard)).

Two other sheets support the pitch. **Trial by Design Part 3** wants every fact stored once with a version history and links to everywhere it is used, so a change shows exactly what it affects. Its own example changes include a CDISC terminology version update and a new safety finding, each with a different urgency. **Caught in 4K** asks teams to "change one rule mid-trial so your checks must respect effective dates" and to plant unit mix-ups that look like deviations but are data errors ([Regeneron kit](https://regn.link/hackharvard)). The kit's judging notes say "you can tie multiple problems and workflows together", and Signal, Not Noise says teams that connect its sub-problems "stand out". The pitch should therefore lead with one sheet and claim the other two as consequences of the same engine.

| Prompt | Fit | How to frame the agent for it |
|---|---|---|
| Signal, Not Noise | Primary | A monitoring agent that knows what changed since the last data cut. It re-scores only new or newly invalidated records and keeps the review plan and site answers in sync with the protocol version in force. |
| Trial by Design Part 3 | Strong second | Treat protocol versions, dictionary versions and the site roster as governed, versioned facts, and compute the impact of each change on collected data and checks. |
| Caught in 4K | Strong third | Judge each visit against the protocol version in force for that participant on that date, separating true deviations from version artifacts. |
| No P-Hacking Allowed | Stretch add-on | Add an "is the SAP still consistent with protocol v3?" check, since the sheet plants "an amendment the SAP doesn't reflect". |
| MA2 / MA5, Digital Twins, Control Tower | Avoid as primary | MA2 and MA5 are about Medical Affairs content, not running studies. Control Tower's own sheet warns teams won't have access to the systems involved. |

Two cautions. First, Regeneron says that taking starter sheets literally and "just dump[ing] into an agentic build" has generally failed. The team should name a concrete user (the medical monitor or clinical data manager) and a concrete moment (data cut N+1 after Amendment 3) rather than pitch a platform. Second, more starter sheets are released at kickoff, so someone should read them in the first hour in case a better fit appears ([Regeneron kit](https://regn.link/hackharvard)). Multi-year long-term follow-up (LTFU) and registries appear in no sheet. They are the team's own extension and belong in the "why this matters" and roadmap slides, not the core claim.

## Long studies fail silently through four channels

Long studies rarely fail through one dramatic error. They fail through changes that each make sense locally but are never recorded as events that matter for the analysis. The first channel is **people**. A meta-analysis of 143 longitudinal cohorts found **mean retention of 73.5%**, and study duration did not predict retention, so design and management matter more than elapsed time. Offering alternative data-collection modes was associated with **86.1% vs 76.3%** median retention ([Teague et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6258319)). In CAR-T long-term follow-up, **20% of patients treated more than a year earlier had stopped attending visits**, and 53% lived more than two hours from their treatment site ([JITC 2026](https://jitc.bmj.com/content/14/4/e013878)). Attrition is often informative. The oncology estimands working group recommends recording a reason for every missed assessment and treating loss to follow-up as potentially treatment-related unless there is a clear rationale otherwise ([Siegel et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01781)).

The second channel is **measurement**. In ADNI-1, **about 35% of 773 participants had a scanner change**. A Philips-to-Siemens switch produced a spurious 1.81% whole-brain volume offset, larger than the 1.69% a year of atrophy seen in Alzheimer's disease ([Lee et al. 2019](https://adni.loni.usc.edu/adni-publications/Estimating%20and%20accounting%20for%20the%20effect%20of%20MRI%20scanner%20changes%20on%20longitudinal%20whole-brain%20volume%20change%20measurements.pdf)). In UK primary care, the move to standardised creatinine created eGFR step changes in **23.7% of 20,000 patient series and 63% of practices**. Almost all of them made kidney function look better, and they fell exactly on the switch date ([Poh et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.1542)). NHANES needed official correction equations for the same problem across survey cycles ([CDC](https://Www.Cdc.gov/nchs/data/nhanes/nhanes_03_04/general_%20note_for_serum_creatinine.pdf)). These artifacts can point the same way as a true effect, so they bias results rather than just adding noise.

The third channel is **codes and dictionaries**. MedDRA updates twice a year and WHODrug quarterly. Up-versioning can deactivate terms or move them to a different system organ class, which regroups adverse events that were already coded ([SCDM GCDMP](https://scdm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Medical-Coding-Dictionary-Management-Maintenance.pdf)). Up-versioning partway through a study can force 100% recoding when the structure changes ([Precision for Medicine](https://www.precisionformedicine.com/blog/when-to-up-version-your-meddra-and-whodrug-dictionaries/)). At the US switch from ICD-9 to ICD-10, opioid-related inpatient stays **rose 18.3% in a single quarter**, a jump AHRQ could not separate from real change ([AHRQ HCUP](https://hcup-us.ahrq.gov/datainnovations/ICD-10CaseStudyonOpioid-RelatedIPStays042417.pdf)).

The fourth channel is **protocols**. In the 2024 Tufts wave, amended protocols averaged **3.3 substantial amendments**. Getting each one from identified need to approval took 260 days on average. The first patient was re-consented after 89 days, 2.5 times the 2010 figure. The latest cost data (2016) put the median direct cost of a substantial amendment at **$141k in Phase II and $535k in Phase III** ([IntuitionLabs/Tufts](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/clinical-trial-protocol-amendments-cost-data)). These figures come through an aggregator, though the kit confirms the 2016 costs. Operational continuity sits underneath all four channels. FDA's cell and gene therapy office has said it may need help making sure 15-year follow-up actually gets done, citing "physician retirements, company closures" ([Pink Sheet 2023](https://insights.citeline.com/PS149041/US-FDA-Struggling-With-Long-Term-Follow-Up-Requirements-For-Gene-Therapies/)).

The fixes for each channel are already known: scanner covariates in mixed models, calibration equations, recoding everything to one dictionary version, and estimand sensitivity analyses. **Every one of them assumes someone recorded when and where each change happened.** That event log, kept continuously and linked to every affected data point, is the agent's job.

## Gene therapy follow-up and amended trials need it most

The use-case ranking below is the team's own inference from the evidence above. No source provides it. The first three settings combine long duration, documented artifacts and high regulatory stakes.

| Rank | Setting | Why it drifts | What the agent does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gene and cell therapy LTFU (up to 15 years) | Required registry plus annual vector-persistence testing ([ProPharma on FDA 2020 guidance](https://www.propharmagroup.com/blog/a-guide-to-understanding-long-term-follow-up-for-gene-therapy-clinical-trials/)); 20% of CAR-T patients stop follow-up after a year; sites manually transcribe EHR data into up to about 14 registry forms ([JITC 2026](https://jitc.bmj.com/content/14/4/e013878)) | Due and overdue tracking per patient, flags when a patient's site or physician changes, assay-change log, registry pre-fill with provenance, continuity record if the sponsor exits |
| 2 | Alzheimer's, neurodegeneration and imaging | Scanner and sequence changes worth about a year of atrophy ([Lee et al.](https://adni.loni.usc.edu/adni-publications/Estimating%20and%20accounting%20for%20the%20effect%20of%20MRI%20scanner%20changes%20on%20longitudinal%20whole-brain%20volume%20change%20measurements.pdf)) | Watch DICOM headers, build the scanner-covariate table, alert when a change has too few participants to model |
| 3 | Any multi-year trial with amendments (the hackathon demo) | 3.3 amendments per amended protocol and 215 days on mismatched versions ([Tufts via IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/clinical-trial-protocol-amendments-cost-data)) | Judge each visit against the protocol version in force, show what each amendment invalidates, keep review checks and site answers current |
| 4 | Oncology overall-survival follow-up | Informative loss to follow-up and treatment crossover ([Siegel et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01781)) | Chase vital status, prompt sites for reason codes, track intercurrent events against the estimand |
| 5 | CKD and diabetes outcome trials | Creatinine method and eGFR equation changes ([Poh et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.1542)) | Change-point detection per lab, propose a calibration bridge |
| 6 | Post-marketing and Phase IV registries | 54% of Phase IV protocols amended; dictionary versioning regroups safety signals ([Tufts](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/clinical-trial-protocol-amendments-cost-data); [SCDM](https://scdm.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Medical-Coding-Dictionary-Management-Maintenance.pdf)) | Record dictionary version per record, recode to one version for pooled analyses |

The best demo setting is not the most compelling story. Gene therapy LTFU makes the strongest "why it matters" slide, because the timeline is 15 years, FDA has stated concern, and patients are visibly lost. But its value lies in operations: contact tracking and registry transcription. That is hard to show convincingly with synthetic data in 36 hours. Rank 3 is exactly what Signal, Not Noise describes and can be scored against an answer key. The team should **demo rank 3 and pitch ranks 1 and 2 as where the same engine goes next**. Rare-disease registries, pediatric cohorts, open-label extensions and vaccine durability studies are plausible extensions, but the research found no sourced figures for them. Keep them off the slides or label them as untested ideas.

## Deterministic detectors, a narrow LLM and a bitemporal study memory

Published systems from 2025 and 2026 converge on one architecture: **thin deterministic tools, thick reasoning, human sign-off**. ClinAgent exposes data-reading and spec tools to coding agents over MCP. It wraps them in audit logging and PHI masking, and its author says expert review is mandatory because LLM-generated derivations were only 72.1% accurate overall ([PMC13555824](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13555824/)). Roche's harmonisation platform for 511 studies sends ambiguous cases to an LLM and everything else to a rule engine with a terminology service, with change control on the rules ([medRxiv 2026](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.04.26345603v1.full)). The case against letting a model produce codes on its own is stark. Without a vocabulary tool, GPT-4o-class models got **0% of OMOP concept lookups right**, and 10.4% of the IDs they gave did not exist. With an Athena lookup tool behind MCP, retrieval reached 100%, and the share of completely wrong mappings was 8.5%, against 24.6% for human mappers ([arXiv 2509.03828](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.03828)). The best published result in the closest application area was Octozi's LLM-plus-rules review of adverse-event discrepancies. It reported accuracy rising from 45.3% to 91.5% and false positives falling from 48.0% to 3.1%, though on synthetic data with vendor authors ([arXiv 2508.05519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05519)).

For this agent, the pattern breaks into five parts.

**Study memory.** A small store with **two time axes per fact** (bitemporal): when a fact was valid and when it was recorded. It covers protocol versions as structured rules (USDM-lite JSON with eligibility cutoffs, visit windows and dose rules), the unit convention and lab vendor per site, the dictionary version, and the site roster.

**Detectors.** Deterministic Python functions that return JSON:
- `ruptures` PELT change points on monthly lab means per site.
- Wave-to-wave KS or PSI tests via scipy or `evidently`.
- A `statsmodels` mixed model with a post-change indicator, to size any step in clinical units.
- Site KRI z-scores modelled on Gilead's open-source `gsm` package, which computes 12 site-level KRIs ([GitHub](https://github.com/gilead-biostats/gsm)).
- A code-frequency shift check for dictionary changes.

**Reasoning layer.** Claude, via the Agent SDK or tool use, reads amendment text and drafts the rule diff. It calls the detectors, then attributes each detected change to an entry in study memory, or flags it as "unexplained". **Attribution is the product.** Detecting drift is easy. Telling a creatinine step caused by a vendor switch apart from real kidney decline is the work.

**Approval queue.** No rule change, calibration or updated answer takes effect without a named reviewer's approval.

**Audit trail.** Append-only and hash-chained. It records every tool call, input hash, model and prompt version, rationale and decision, following Harmonia's provenance database ([arXiv 2502.07132](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.07132v3)) and ClinAgent's audit layer.

The regulatory framing follows from this design. ICH E6(R3) moves sponsors toward risk-based quality management, critical-to-quality factors, end-to-end data governance and audit trails ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ich-e6-r3-gcp-updates-implementation)). One source puts the EU effective date at 23 July 2025. Another says FDA issued final guidance in September 2025, which the team should check before quoting it. FDA's January 2025 draft AI guidance asks for a context-of-use and model-risk statement backed by credibility evidence ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/01/alerts-lifesciences-aiml-fda-publishes-its-first-draft-guidance)). The pitch can map directly onto it. Context of use: flags potential data-integrity issues for data managers. Model influence: low, because a human reviews every flag. Credibility evidence: precision and recall on the planted-event benchmark. The team should claim alignment with these frameworks, never compliance.

## Judges will test readiness and the incumbent question

Two rubrics apply. Regeneron scores **Utility & Relevance, Readiness, and Design**. It generally considers only projects with a GitHub repo, ideally MIT-licensed, plus a demo video of about 3 minutes and a deck of 10 to 12 slides. HackHarvard scores Innovation/Impact, Technical Complexity, Functionality/Usability and Teamwork equally, breaking ties in that order ([Regeneron kit](https://regn.link/hackharvard)). Every Regeneron sheet sets the same readiness bar: measured against an answer key and a baseline, protects blinding, keeps an audit trail, runs from a clean clone, and states its limits honestly. Utility is the idea's strength, because the amendment and dictionary statistics are easy to cite. **Readiness is the main risk.** An abstract "drift" concept that does not run end to end on realistic data will lose to a narrower tool that does.

The hardest judge question will be: "Medidata Detect and CluePoints already do central statistical monitoring." The honest answer is yes. Detect finds "data errors, trends, and anomalies" with no customer programming ([Medidata](https://www.medidata.com/en/press-releases/medidata-announces-launch-of-medidata-detect-its-centralized-statistical-monitoring-solution-for-clinical-trials)). CluePoints covers RBQM, site oversight and AI medical coding ([CluePoints](https://cluepoints.com/about-us/)). Saama and IQVIA sell agentic data review ([Saama](https://saama.com/fact-sheet/agentic-ai-fact-sheet); [BusinessWire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20250611916965/en/IQVIA-Launches-New-AI-Agents-for-Life-Sciences-and-Healthcare)). Veeva's new Study Builder Agent turns a protocol into an EDC build in as little as 24 hours ([BioXconomy](https://www.bioxconomy.com/clinical-and-research/veeva-unveils-ai-agent-to-speed-clinical-trial-study-configuration)). None of their public materials prominently describes the follow-on step: reading Amendment N and reworking already-collected data, derived values, review checks and site answers. Phrase it carefully, as "not prominent in public product materials", because vendors may have features they do not publicise. Position the agent as an orchestration layer on top of these tools, which matches the kit's own view that the near-term gains come from "fixing the process and orchestration around them". Never use cross-site outlier statistics as the headline, because incumbents own that space.

Prepare answers for four more objections. On data: the base is CDISC pilot SDTM plus a scripted time machine and planted events, and the deck should say so plainly. On hallucination: the LLM drafts rule diffs and explanations only, deterministic code does all flagging, and a human approves every change. On blinding: arm-level drift is never computed by default. On scope: five drift channels is five problems, so build depth on three (protocol amendment, unit or lab-vendor change, dictionary up-version) and show the rest only as roadmap.

## Conclusion

The core insight is that the hard part of longitudinal accuracy is mostly not statistics. Change-point tests, calibration equations and scanner covariates have existed for years. What breaks is the **link between a data point and the context it was collected under**. Each failure in this report, from the ADNI scanner offsets to the eGFR steps to 215 days on a stale protocol, was fixable once someone knew when and where the change happened. The project should therefore present itself as a memory-and-attribution system with detectors attached, not as a better anomaly detector. That framing is also what separates it from Medidata and CluePoints.

Two uncertainties should shape how the team pitches. No public benchmark exists for drift detection in trials, so the team's own planted-event metrics will be the only evidence, and judges should hear both what the agent missed and what it caught. And the highest-impact setting, 15-year gene therapy follow-up, depends mainly on keeping patients and records connected, which a synthetic demo cannot prove. A team that wins with the amendment demo and names LTFU as its next problem gives an honest answer that is also more credible.
